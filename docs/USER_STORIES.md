# User stories

**The user is the production manager of a small or mid-sized manufacturer.** Orders, stock, machines and staff are usually coordinated with spreadsheets and experience. BYOF Agent turns each disruption into response options that can be compared, lets the manager decide the trade-offs, and then carries out the approved business operations. The disruption simulator stands in for the shop floor: it creates disruptions for demonstrations and tests, and does not do the resupply or repair work after the manager approves.

| Story | What the manager needs to get done | Expected behavior |
|---|---|---|
| US-01 Today's production | Know what is scheduled today and which orders are at risk | Demand, qualified completion, work in progress and plan forecasts are verified; the CP-SAT schedule is checked by an independent Checker, and an unfinished search is never presented as a proof of infeasibility. |
| US-02 Demand change | Keep the economic value after a rush order or quantity change | Concrete combinations of resupply, overtime, a negotiated due date or a smaller quantity are compared, with delivery and net contribution costed for every affected order. When no measure can keep a due date, the options propose a new date for the customer to agree to; any change to a customer order needs the manager's explicit confirmation that the customer agreed. |
| US-03 Shortage | Decide how much to resupply, when, and how much extra spend is worth it | The gap is computed from the BOM; standard, expedited and immediate supply list quantity, timing, cash and surcharges. After approval the supply is received immediately or arrives on the factory clock, without any further step in the simulator. |
| US-04 Machines and staff | Judge the cost of repair, cover and rescheduling | Existing resources or measures with explicit qualifications and lead times are used, executed work is protected, and unknown remaining work is never invented: the shop floor confirms it, and the waiting conversation then continues by itself. |
| US-05 Cost and preference | See the trade-offs between profit, cash, delivery and stability | Everything is costed over the same window and set of orders without counting regular material cost twice; an explicit request in the conversation beats saved preferences, and a budget limit is never broken by soft ranking. Only real, differing options are shown, never padding and never a claim of the globally cheapest plan. |
| US-06 Approval and execution | Agree once after reviewing an option and let the Agent do the work | The actions, cost and delivery scope are frozen at approval, executed through idempotent business commands and checked again against the schedule. Material changes in the facts or a scope overrun stop execution and ask the manager; retries within the original approval never buy twice. |
| US-07 Failure and cancellation | Know how far execution got and avoid spending twice | The execution ledger separates applied measures, pending receipts, pending release and factory acceptance; retryable steps can continue and unfinished steps can be cancelled. Receipts or costs that already happened are never shown as refunded. |
| US-08 Continuous conversation | Keep discussing after refreshing or switching conversations | Messages, tool activity, comparisons and receipts are attributed in time order; drafts are kept and earlier messages can be loaded. Stopping an analysis blocks automatic continuation but does not stop the factory or undo approved actions. |
| US-09 Model choice | Choose the model for one's own conversations | Settings only offer the models configured on the server; the choice applies to later turns of the account, a running turn keeps its model, and nothing switches silently. |

Prices, supply, repair and staff measures come from a versioned price catalog in SGD; net contribution is not accounting profit. A qualified completion is not a shipment or customer acceptance, and customer agreement is a confirmation by the manager, not an external contract.

Email notifications, real purchasing, ERP/MES integration, automatic adaptation to any factory, multi-organization identity and order-level split-delivery contracts are outside the current scope. The adverse cost case is a transparent financial sensitivity, not a probability forecast or a second schedule. Solving has time limits and does not guarantee a feasible plan for every combination of disruptions.
